A hemp sale depends on several things being true at once

The lab report is current. The listing matches it. The stock exists. The buyer is 21, and someone checked their ID. In most stores each of those lives somewhere different: a folder of PDFs, a menu plugin, a point of sale, a clipboard.

  • A listing outlives its lab report

    Nothing ties the expiry date on a PDF to the product page, so keeping them in step depends on someone remembering.

  • The counter can’t see the order’s history

    Was it paid? Is the stock still set aside? Did the buyer confirm their age? The answers are in three different tools.

  • Every change is a manual sweep

    When a delivery method has to stop or a product has to come down, it gets done tool by tool, by hand.

Green Loom keeps the product, its lab record, the order and the handoff on one record, so each step starts with what the last one recorded.

One batch, from lab report to the counter

Follow a single product through the app. Pick any step to watch it.

  1. Record the batch: enter the lab report once, and stock arrives as lots under it.
  2. Publish the listing: a product goes on the menu once it has a price and a checked lab record.
  3. Customer confirms 21+: at checkout the shopper confirms they are 21 or older.
  4. Work the order: accept it, pack it, mark it ready.
  5. Hand it off: staff confirm they checked a government ID and collected payment.

Sample data. Screens are simplified; the labels and status words are the app’s own.

The work around the sale

Two jobs that usually mean a spreadsheet or a scramble.

See the profit under every price

A price book sets prices for a whole category: tiers down the side, sizes across. Enter what a gram costs and each price shows what it earns.

  • Round prices to the cent, up to the next $5, or down to the $5 below
  • A price typed on one product is pinned, and later changes to the book skip it
Sample: entering a cost per gram shows the profit under each price.

Pause one delivery method, not the store

When delivery can’t run, pause it and write the notice shoppers see. The other methods stay open, and orders already placed aren’t affected.

  • Start now or later, with an optional time to resume
  • A legal closure stops all ordering; reopening takes a reason and two different people
Sample: pausing local delivery leaves pickup and shipping open.

The checks happen because of how the work is done

When these steps share a record, nobody has to be the link between them. A product can’t be published without a checked, in-date lab record if the store requires one. A pickup or local delivery can’t be completed until staff confirm the ID check.

And when a question comes up later, the order’s age check names the staff member who looked at the ID and when.

Where the edges are today

  • Age is confirmed by the shopper at checkout and by staff checking ID in person. Green Loom does not verify identity online.
  • Staff record payment at handoff. There is no card processing yet.
  • Courier names and tracking numbers are typed in. There is no carrier integration yet.
  • Green Loom records these checks. It does not make a store compliant on its own.
